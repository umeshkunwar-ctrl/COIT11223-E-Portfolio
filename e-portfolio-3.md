The reference group was established to explore current and emerging copyright issues created by Artificial Intelligence and to identify possible legal uncertainties and regulatory gaps. **Source:** World Intellectual Property Organization – *Generative AI: Navigating Intellectual Property*

### Summary of the Artefact

The World Intellectual Property Organization published *Generative AI: Navigating Intellectual Property* in 2024. The publication provides guiding principles and a checklist to help organisations understand intellectual property risks when adopting generative AI. It encourages organisations to ask appropriate questions and consider safeguards before using generative AI technologies (World Intellectual Property Organization, 2024).

## Artefact 4 – Copyright in Australia: Key Idea from Workshop 7

### Summary of the Artefact

A key idea I selected from the Week 7 workshop is copyright protection in Australia. The workshop explained that copyright provides exclusive rights over original works and protects the way information is expressed rather than the information itself. Copyright protection is automatic and does not require registration. Protected material can include computer programs, photographs, artistic works, music, sound recordings, broadcasts and films (CQUniversity Australia, 2026).

### Reflection and Justification

I selected this concept because copyright is directly relevant to ICT professionals who regularly work with software and digital content. **[PERSONALISE: Write one sentence explaining what you personally found most useful about this part of Workshop 7.]** I learned that finding material online does not automatically mean that I have permission to copy or reuse it. In my future ICT projects, I will pay closer attention to copyright ownership, licences, permission and attribution before using material created by another person.
